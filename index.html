About Chandrayaan-3

Chandrayaan-3 is India's third lunar exploration mission under the Chandrayaan program. It successfully demonstrated India's capability for safe and soft landing on the lunar surface, specifically near the unexplored south pole.

The mission consists of a lander module named Vikram, a rover module named Pragyan, and a Propulsion Module. Vikram carried Pragyan to the moon surface, which was later deployed to conduct in-situ chemical analyses and scientific experiments.

With this monumental achievement, ISRO has not only secured India's place as a global space superpower but also gathered crucial lunar data that will assist space agencies worldwide in future manned landings.

Launch Phase

Chandrayaan-3 launched successfully aboard LVM3-M4 rocket from Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC) SHAR, Sriharikota, placing the spacecraft in precise Earth orbit.

MISSION COMPONENTS

The Three Pillars

Vikram Lander

The spacecraft designed to touch down on the lunar surface. It houses science payloads to study thermal conductivity and moonquakes.

Pragyan Rover

A 6-wheeled solar-powered exploration rover designed to navigate up to 500 meters and conduct in-situ chemical composition checks.

Propulsion Module

The module responsible for conveying the lander and rover combination from injection orbit up to 100km lunar orbit before separation.
